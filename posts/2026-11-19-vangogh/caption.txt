$190.4 million. For a painting by a man who sold almost nothing while he was alive. 🦅

This week at Sotheby's in New York, Van Gogh's "Chestnut Trees in Flower" (1890) set a new record for the artist, beating the old one by $73M.

Same canvas. Same paint. What changed was the story, and what people were willing to pay for it.

3 pricing lessons from the auction room:
→ Sell the story, not the specs. People pay more for what they can retell.
→ Make it scarce. Abundance kills perceived value.
→ Remove every doubt. A dispute over the painting's history was settled the day before the sale. Trust cleared the way for the bid.
